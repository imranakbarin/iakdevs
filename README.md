# Infinite World Scrolls

A standalone canvas experience at https://imranakbarin.github.io/iakdevs/.

## Today (home screen)

A visit without a link opens **Today** (`assets/home.js`): a card per experience with a small moving preview (Pip running through his kingdom, World art cycling through traditions, the touring car on the road, the garden, the reading nook) and what you have done there, read from what each experience already keeps on the device: Pip's best on today's world, the sights found (of 15) and landmarks in his journey book, the World art wonders seen (of 31), Road trip postcards and roadside stops, the Zen garden's season and whether you have raked it, and the last Cozy room. Tap a card to step in; **⌂ Today** in the experience menu (and in Cozy Worlds' own menu) brings you back, and Esc or ✕ returns to where you were. The scene behind pauses while it is open. Links to an experience still go straight there; `#today` opens it.

## Modes

- **World art** — endless living paintings: Gond, Warli, Madhubani, Kangra, Japanese, Chinese and Mexican folk-art inspired. Live art uses each tradition's procedural chunk grammar; Artworks → Painting uses the authored panoramas in `assets/art-*-living.webp` (see `assets/living-art-notes.md`).
- **Road trip** — distance-based perspective road with weather, five environments, a touring car and at most one other vehicle. An animated drive, not a traffic simulator.
- **Zen garden** — raked-sand patterns over painted gardens.
- **Cozy Worlds** — calming illustrated scenes.
- **Pip Run** — a small original platformer you can play (`#pip-run`).
- **Toy Town** — a chaotic isometric toy town, themed every day by the news (`#toy-town`).

## Toy Town

`toy-town.html` + `assets/toy-town.js` (`#toy-town`, or Toy Town in the menu): a busy little isometric town on a slab of earth, where something silly is always happening. A new town is laid out every day (seeded by the date; *New town* makes a random one) with roads, a river and bridges, houses, shops, towers, a fire station, a park, a building site with a turning crane and the day's theme block. Cars queue and honk on their own, people walk the pavements, ducks paddle, and chaos overlaps: burst hydrants, runaway shopping carts, a dog with the butcher's sausages, escaping balloons, a cat on a roof (the fire truck comes; the cat jumps down by itself), pigeons, banana peels, an ice cream van, a traffic jam, escaped chickens, a giant rubber duck, and now and then a UFO borrowing a car. The camera holds the town still (no zooming after events); drag to look around (handy on phones, where the town is wider than the screen), a ticker reports what is happening, and a tap pokes the town. A five-minute day ends in a night of lit windows and street lamps.

**The day's theme comes from the news.** The page reads Wikipedia's "In the news" for the day (`api.wikimedia.org` featured feed, cached per day) and turns up to three light stories, each a different theme, into the town's day; the first leads it and builds its block. The specific kind of story picks what happens, from 36 variants: cricket (a SIX! out of the stadium, a dog after the ball), tennis, a Grand Prix (racers lapping the middle block), a marathon, a medal podium, football; a rocket to the Moon (a Moon in the sky), a Mars rover in the park, a comet; a polling queue or a rally; Diwali (lamps along every street, a rangoli, rising lanterns, fireworks), Christmas (a tree, snow, a sleigh), Halloween (pumpkins, little ghosts, bats), a New Year countdown, Holi colour clouds, Eid lanterns and sweets; a concert, a prize podium, a red carpet; a phone launch (everyone looking down and bumping), robots, delivery drones; a panda, a whale in the river, a friendly dinosaur strolling down the street, a penguin parade, a zoo escape; coins raining from the bank tower, Market Mania, the lab; and rain, snow or wind on days without news. Any story mentioning deaths, war, attacks, disasters, crime and the like is never used (whole words and word beginnings, so "fireworks" is fine and "fire" is not); with no usable story, or offline, the date picks the day. The card names the theme, the story it came from and what else is in town. Everything runs at 60% speed so it is easy to follow.

**Soft and close to the news.** Everything is drawn soft, like clay toys: gradient faces, rounded bevelled edges with a highlight, soft contact shadows, trees as shaded swaying balls, rounded people and little wheels. The day's real headlines are in town: the *Toy Town Times* billboard stands on the grass at the town's right-hand corner (only sky behind it, so it hides no houses) and shows them in turn, a small plane tows the headline across the sky, neighbours stop on the pavement to chat about the story by name ("Did you see India play?", "Happy Diwali!"), newspaper kiosks stand on free corners, the streets get bunting on match, festival, election and premiere days, buses show where everyone is going (STADIUM, FESTIVAL, POLLS…), and on the night of a big story windows flicker blue with the telly; a newspaper seller walks the streets calling "Extra!" with them, the ticker repeats them every half minute, and each news scene flies a banner with the story's own name (its longest run of capitalised words: "GO INDIA!", "HAPPY DIWALI!", "ARTEMIS III 🚀", "WELCOME, …!") while a few fans carry it on signs.

**A calm town.** Seven cars, four cyclists and fourteen walkers share the streets, and traffic lights at every crossing let each direction take its turn (with an amber in between); cars, bikes and walkers all wait at red, keep their distance and never honk. On its own the town has gentle moments, one every few seconds and never more than three at once: a kite in the park, a picnic, someone watering the window boxes (flowers appear), a painter at an easel, a quiet read on a bench, butterflies, a cat napping on a warm roof, one red balloon drifting away, the street sweeper, the ice cream van, a busker, a hot air balloon, pigeons, the marching band, the rubber duck. The noisier pranks (UFO, runaway carts, burst hydrants…) are no longer part of the everyday rotation, and a tap brings a balloon, butterflies, a kite, pigeons or a busker.

## Deep links

`#<style>-<seed>` opens a style at a seed, e.g. `#gond-12345`, `#warli-7`. Prefix `painting-` for the painted panorama (`#painting-kangra-3`). `#zen-…`, `#road-…` and `#cozy-…` open the other modes; `#today` (or no hash) opens the home screen. Seeds vary the starting layout; they do not regenerate painted panoramas. Editing the hash in an open tab loads that world.

## Gond benchmark

`assets/gond-benchmark.js` replaces the Gond chunk grammar with a composition-first layout: one hero tree per chapter, an optional support tree whose placement is planned from a separate seeded stream so neighbouring chunks never collide, one animal story, rooted bank plants and patterned distant rises that run down behind the bank.

## Creature behaviour

`assets/creature-mind.js` gives live-art creatures simple intentions. `wander` alternates rests (with an idle action) and purposeful walks between seeded waypoints; `flight` tours perches for birds and butterflies. Both are pure functions of time and seed, so paused redraws stay pixel-identical and evicted chunks rebuild exactly; gait phase follows distance actually covered, so nothing moonwalks.

- **Gond:** each chapter's animal keeps to its own chapter by half its body width (no cross-seam overlaps). Deer graze, rabbits nibble, peacocks shiver their fans and peck; prey next to a tiger chapter stops, faces and watches it while the tiger crouches into a stalk. Birds commute between the real crowns of neighbouring trees (crown geometry is replayed from the tree painter's seed, which `add()` now returns).
- **Warli / Madhubani / Kangra:** grazing animals and strollers walk-and-rest; walkers' and the plough team's legs stop at the turn. Kangra's boat moors and faces its direction of travel. Madhubani zone names now match what they paint.
- **Japan / China / Mexico:** flocks fly the way they face instead of flapping in place; a butterfly works each Mexican clearing's flowers and pond edge.
- **Road trip:** props fade with the share of their place in the ground under them, and roadside slots cross-fade to the new place, so the desert no longer carries alpine pines.

## Presence (calm interaction)

`assets/art-presence.js` lets live art respond to the viewer without new controls; every response fades within a few seconds and reduced motion disables it.

- **Attention:** a resting mouse pointer, or a finger held still for ~0.4s, is noticed by nearby creatures. Their own clock eases to a stop, they look up and face you, then resume from where they were (no jumps). Gond prey prick up; the tiger watches; a watched peacock displays.
- **Lean in:** while you attend, the scroll eases to ~30% speed, then returns.
- **Tap:** water ripples; fish, ducks and hens scatter and drift back; a tap near a perched Gond bird sends it to another tree; Mexican butterflies are shy of a close pointer.

Inputs are recorded on the scene clock, so paused redraws remain pixel-identical.

## Ambience

`assets/art-ambience.js` adds slow mist veils, cloud shadows and water glints to the painterly live-art styles (Kangra, Japan, China, Mexico). Mushroom Island's `assets/mushroom-life.js` animates the painted kingdom (mist, lamps, townsfolk, flocks, light shafts) and gives Pip's run star seeds to collect, a fairy companion and butterflies that lift off as he passes.

## World art: the living gallery

`assets/art-gallery.js` shows the painted panoramas as an exhibition, and World art now opens on them (a link to a live style such as `#gond-12` still opens live art; Artworks → Live art switches). On larger screens each painting hangs in a gilt frame on a lit wall with a museum plaque; phones show it full bleed. The painting eases with the pointer or the phone's tilt and a near layer of motes, petals or fireflies drifts faster than it as it pans. The light follows the local clock: rosy dawn, soft shafts by day, golden hour, violet dusk, moonlight with stars and fireflies at night. Every couple of minutes the tour glides on to the next tradition. *Frame on the wall* and *Tour the traditions* toggle in Artworks (kept in `localStorage['art-gallery']`). It wraps `LivingArt.draw`; the paintings and their own motion are unchanged.

## World art: richer live paintings

`assets/art-richer.js` fills each live tradition the way its real paintings fill the frame, in its own idiom: Gond gets a patterned leaf-rayed sun (a moon in dark mode), dash-filled clouds and flocks of dotted birds; Warli the ray-circled sun and moon, V-stroke birds, ranges of dotted triangle hills and little huts; Kangra soft clouds (blurred once into a single shape) and birds; Japan gold kasumi mist bands and cranes; China pale ink-wash peaks, mist bands and skeins of geese; Mexico patterned amate birds. Madhubani, already dense, is unchanged. All of it is added to each chunk as static layers (seeded per chunk, painted once into the cached tiles, so no per-frame cost), after the tradition's own grammar.

## World art wonders

`assets/art-wonders.js` cuts each endless live landscape into long stretches (about a minute and a half of drifting). Most hold one rare sight, seeded and never the same twice in a row: a migrating flock, kites on the wind (strings down to the land), or a passing shower that leaves a rainbow once the cloud has gone by; fireflies when the theme is dark; petals on the wind in Japan, rising lanterns in China and papel picado garlands in Mexico. Each is drawn in the style's own ink and palette (Warli in white line), as a pure function of seed, stretch and scene time, so a paused redraw is unchanged. A quiet italic line under the style's name says what is passing. `tests/art-wonders.test.cjs` checks the no-repeat rule, every kind per style and deterministic drawing.

**Wonders seen.** The first sighting of each kind in each tradition is kept on this device (`localStorage['art-wonders:seen']`) and marked "· new in your wonders" under the style's name; the Artworks dialog ends with a *Wonders seen* section, one row per tradition (31 in all; fireflies need dark mode). Live captions now read like the gallery: Gond, Warli, Madhubani, Kangra, Japanese woodblock, Chinese ink, Mexican amate, with the country in the line below.

## Zen garden: raking and visitors

Drag across the sand to rake it: a five-tine rake follows your finger or pointer, carved with the same ridges and troughs as the steel ball's trail (a quiet hint says so on the first visit), and the garden renews with the next pattern as usual. A tap on the water still ripples it. About once a minute a small bird lands on the rim of the sand bed, hops in leaving tiny footprints, pecks and flies off (tap near it and it leaves sooner); by day a dragonfly hovers and darts over the lily pads. Raking by hand has its own sand hiss when sound is on.

**Seasons** (`assets/zen-seasons.js`). The garden opens in the calendar's season and turns every two and a half minutes of garden time (a paused garden holds still), a short line naming each: blossom drifting in spring; warm motes and butterflies in summer; maple leaves in autumn that settle on the sand until the ball rolls through them or the rake sweeps them off; snow, a dusting on the sand and frost at the edges in winter. Butterflies settle on the sand and lift off when the ball, the rake or a tap comes near (not in the moonlit garden); in autumn and winter a crane glides over, its shadow crossing the sand.

## Road trip journey

`assets/road-journey.js` turns the drive into a trip between named places (original names). Each stretch heads for a town in the next landscape and the status line counts down to it ("Saltwind Bay in 1.2 km"). Arriving, as the new landscape takes over, shows a postcard: the town, how far you came from the last one and the weather on the way, stamped into a trip log kept on this device ("Stamp 6 of 20"). With a fixed place, towns come every stretch within it. It only reads the road's state.

**Stops and the horn** (`assets/road-stops.js`). When a roadside stop is coming up (a café, a campsite, a windmill, an overlook…), a tap on the road pulls over: the car brakes smoothly and parks with the stop beside the road ahead (only stops that will be drawn there are offered, never mid-overtake), hazards blinking while other traffic flows past. The place cycle and the trip's countdown wait; a card names the stop and keeps it in the trip log (15 to find); a tap, or fourteen seconds, drives on. A tap on the car (or H) sounds a two-tone horn. Arriving in a town, the car eases off with a soft chime (sound on) and "Welcome to …" rises over the road with its postcard.

## Soundscapes

`assets/world-sound.js` gives World art, Road trip and Zen garden sound, synthesized with WebAudio (no audio files). Road trip: an engine that follows the speed and pulls harder when overtaking, road and wind noise, rain, night crickets, waves on the coast, birds in the forest, a far horn in the city and a whoosh as an oncoming car passes. Zen garden: sand hissing under the steel ball (it follows the ball's speed), the bamboo fountain, a splash where you tap the pond, now and then a wind chime or a singing bowl, crickets and frogs by moonlight. World art: river and wind beds, birds and insects, and every so often a short phrase in a voice suited to the tradition (a breathy flute for the Indian styles, plucked strings for Japan and China, a guitar-like pluck for Mexico). Off by default: **♪** in the bar (or M); the choice is remembered and resumes on the first tap. Cozy Worlds, Mushroom Island and Pip Run keep their own sound, so the button hides there. `tests/world-sound.test.cjs` covers both modules.

**Soft by design.** Both synthesizers (`world-sound.js`, `pip-audio.js`) pass everything through a gentle high shelf (−7 dB above 2.6 kHz), a lowpass and a small dark room, so notes bloom instead of click. Chimes ring in whole-number partials (the glassy 2.76×/5.4× partials are gone) and an octave lower; plucked voices (koto, zheng, guitar, Pip's score) are filtered strings that darken after the attack; birds sit below 3 kHz and crickets below 4 kHz, quieter; rain is soft noise instead of high ticks; the Zen sand hiss moved from ~2.8 kHz to ~1 kHz; no attack is shorter than ~10 ms; melodies stay within an octave and a half. Measured over 40 s per scene in headless Chromium, the share of energy above 3 kHz fell from 66% to 9% in the Zen garden and from about 15–20% to 4–5% in World art; Road trip and Pip Run lost 13–16 dB above 3 kHz. Cozy Worlds' rain is lowpassed at 2.3 kHz (was 4.2).

## Pip Run

`pip-run.html` + `assets/pip-run.js` (~40 KB of source, 13 KB gzipped) is a real game rather than an animation:

- **Hero feel:** the painted Pip with classic platformer feel: squash and stretch, a braced skid pose, a forward lean at speed and distinct take-off/apex/landing poses.
- **Golden Sprout:** some star blocks release a sprout that slides and drops; catching it makes Pip bigger. Big Pip smashes bricks from below and takes one hit (shrinking, with a flicker) instead of tumbling. The planner values bumping star blocks and predicts a sprout's slide-and-drop, so the demo grows in most runs.
- **Biomes with their own rules (release 3):** regions of ~288 m run Mushroom Meadow, then Frost Ridge, Sky Isles and Glow Caves in a seeded order, with Meadow between cycles. Rules live in the shared physics, so the planner adapts without special cases: ice has low friction and acceleration (long skids), the sky has lighter gravity and gusting wind that drifts Pip, and caves are dark, lit only by Pip, crystals, seeds and star blocks (the thought lines glow above the dark). Each biome has its own terrain grammar (floating two-tile islands and spring pads in the sky; a jagged roof, guaranteed headroom and crystals in the caves) and its own tiles, parallax backdrop and weather. Gap tables are computed per biome from the take-off speed a short run-up can build, under a worst-case headwind. The next region's landmark (Mushroom Gate, Frost Spire, Sky Harbor, Crystal Heart) rises on the horizon and grows as Pip approaches, the HUD counts down to it, and a title card announces each region.
- **Director (viewers vs. the AI):** while the demo plays, pick a tool (brick, creature, spring; keys 1 2 3 or the round buttons) and place it just ahead of Pip; 3 charges recharge every 6 s. The planner referees each placement by imagining Pip's futures with it: if every future tumbles, or no surviving future gets past a spot that one could before (a trap), it is refused as "Too cruel". Accepted placements force a visible replan and score: a reroute earns 20–100, a tumble on a fair trap "Gotcha! +150", and Pip using your spring +40. Springs are part of the shared physics, so the AI can use them.

- **A painted world (release 9):** the ground is painted to match Pip rather than built from flat tiles. Earth, stone and stumps never change in play, so each chunk's are painted once into one canvas (`paintChunk`): soil that darkens with depth, strata, pebbles and roots, a grass cap with blades, tufts and flowers that rounds over cliff edges, faces lit from the upper left and shaded on the right, and per-biome dressing (snow lumps, sparkle and icicles; cloud billows with gold veins; glowing moss, glowcaps and stalactites). Stone blocks are bevelled and mossy, each a little different, and columns are tree stumps (snow-capped on the ridge, crystal pillars in the caves). Painted trees, rocks and the odd cottage from `assets/road-painted-props.webp` stand behind the ground on wide flat stretches, graded into each biome. Frost Ridge and Sky Isles get painted backdrops from `assets/road-painted-mountains.webp` (a snowy range with a pine ridge in front; the world far below the clouds), with drifting cumulus and islands that carry trees; the caves get light shafts and glowing crystal clusters. Bricks, star blocks, springs and creatures are re-painted with bevels, gloss, rim light and soft shadows, and a soft sun bloom and vignette (painted once, small, per biome) tie it together. Collision still reads the tile grid, so physics, the planner and ghosts are unchanged. Frame cost stays at the old level: only rows that hold something are kept, and the light is one cached overlay.
- **Leaps from the lip (release 35):** the demo used to walk off an edge, slip a little over the hole and drop a few pixels, then leap from below the lip. It was using the coyote window, the short grace after leaving the ground that lets a late press still jump, which is meant to forgive people. Its planner rated those late leaps as good as any (and sometimes better, a few pixels further on), and gravity here is strong, so a full window drops Pip most of a tile. Now a leap after leaving the ground costs a little in the planner's reckoning, so a leap from the lip wins whenever one does as well. A late one stays for when it is the only way, under a block row that runs up to the edge, say. Late leaps fell from 17% of all leaps to 1.9%, those that dropped more than 4 px from 725 to 55 over 20 runs, and the rest drop less than a pixel. Over 61 runs it tumbles as often as before (1.6 per 12 minutes). `tests/pip-run.test.cjs` fails if more than 5% of the demo's leaps come after walking off the edge (21% did before).
- **Smoother motion (release 34):** the simulation runs at 40 ticks a second and screens draw 60 or 120 frames. Pip's position, the camera and the creatures were already drawn between ticks, but much was not: Pip's stride and squash, the creatures' own clocks (a Mossbud's waddle, a Puddlehop's croak), every particle (dust, sparks, petals, splashes, fluff), and about two dozen drawing animations that read the tick counter (glows, bobbing seeds, the sea's glitter and surf, swaying grass, flickering lamps). They moved in the uneven steps of 40 a second on a faster screen, about three frames on and two off, and that read as stutter. They now all animate from a clock that runs between ticks. Instant replays, played at half speed, are blended between recorded frames too, instead of holding each one. On an iPhone in Low Power Mode, Safari draws at most 30 frames a second, which no page can get past.
- **Clearer instant replays (release 33):** a replay of a close call now shows Pip solid. If he had just respawned he used to blink, and on the blink frames only the see-through ghost of your best run was left, so he read as a dim, bluish Pip. The ghost is left out of replays, and in the caves a replay lifts the dark partway, so the gap or creature he nearly met can be seen. The label says "Instant replay".
- **A sharper demo, and the 3D pass finished (release 32):** the demo player now foresees the creatures by their own rules. Each creature near Pip is played forward once per decision, for the 96 frames a plan looks ahead, without touching the world: walkers and snails turn at walls and ledges and fall off edges, pods burst on what they hit, and a Puddlehop hops on its timer toward where Pip will be. The old guess kept walkers on a straight line, saw a Puddlehop's first hop only, counted an airborne hop's distance twice and aimed it at where Pip stood when the plan was made. Rollouts also bounce off a stomp the way play does (higher while jump is held), let crumbling planks give way under Pip's imagined feet, and know that Pip blinks harmlessly for a while after shrinking (without that, big Pip would not walk past a Puddlehop trapped hopping under a low block row, and dithered). The breeze that carries a stuck Pip on now needs a tile and a half of progress to reset, not a half-step. Over the same 61 runs (28 daily worlds and five seeds, at desktop and phone widths, 12 minutes each) the demo now tumbles 1.6 times per run instead of 4.2 (3.5 before Pip stood on his feet). Pauses over 8 s fell from 53 to 19, and none is longer than the breeze's 15 s. The 3D pass now covers the rest of the world: creatures cast the same soft shadow as Pip on the ground's receding top, following fliers, hoppers and pods into the air; ferries, rafts and lift boards show a receding top and a side; the landmarks stand set back on the plaza's top, in perspective like the faces; and trees, rocks and cottages stand a little back on the ground rather than at its front edge. Shared links use a new format.
- **A touch of 3D, and Pip stands on his feet (release 31):** the world is drawn as if seen in perspective from just above. Every solid tile's exposed top recedes a little into the screen: the lawn, snow, cloud, moss and sand go back behind their painted edge. Blocks, bricks, stones and cliffs show the side that faces the middle of the view, so the faces turn as the view scrolls (left of centre you see right sides, right of centre left sides). Each face takes its colours from its own region's palette or from the painted tile. Pip's shadow lies on the ground's receding top and follows him into the air, smaller and fainter the higher he jumps. All of this is drawn behind the painted tiles and is drawing only. Pip also used to stand at the edge of a hole with his boots over the gap: his collision box is narrower than his painted body, and any sliver of it on the lip held him up. Now he stands on his feet (the middle half of the box). Once they pass the edge he slips off and falls, easing out from the lip, and while they are still just on it he teeters, swaying over the drop with his arms up. Lifts, ferries, springs and crumbling planks go by his feet too. This changes the physics, so shared links use a new format. Tests check he falls with 3 px or less on the lip and stands with 5. The demo tumbles a little more often for it, about 4.2 times per 12 minutes instead of 3.5 over the same 61 runs. It pauses no more than before, and never longer than the breeze's 15 s.
- **No more hanging at a block row (release 30):** reported on the 29 October world at 4614 m in the caves. A row of blocks ended one tile before a two-tile step up, with two Mossbuds patrolling under it. Big Pip could not rise onto the step in that one-tile slot without bumping the step's face or the blocks, so every forward plan ended in a bump or a hit, and the demo jumped back and forth. The breeze that carries a stuck Pip on was meant to wait 30 s, but it was still counted in the old 60-a-second ticks, so it waited 45 s and then moved him ahead, which looked like a restart. Block rows now stop two tiles short of whatever comes next, and the breeze comes after 15 s. A test checks that no block row ends right before a rise; on the previous generator it finds two in seed 1 alone. A stall hunt over 28 daily worlds and five seeds at desktop and phone widths, 12 minutes each, found no hangs. Of 42 pauses longer than 8 s, 39 were Pip walking back from a checkpoint after a tumble, two were waits for a ferry or lift, and one was a cautious 12 s wait for a Puddlehop. The longest was 15 s. The 29 October world now runs past 6000 m. The worlds changed again, so shared links use a new format.
- **Real holes, bursting pods and life in the sea (release 29):** a hole in the ground is now always at least two tiles wide. One-tile holes were narrower than Pip himself, so the little hop over them looked as if he walked across a crack (had he really walked, he would have fallen in). Where only a one-tile jump is safe (a step up, or a headwind in the sky) the far side now comes level instead, and the sky's gaps follow the actual wind along the jump rather than always the worst headwind. Only the floating steps keep their one-tile hops between stones. The worlds changed with it, so shared links use a new format. A stomped seed pod no longer hangs in the air as a flat disc: its husk splits, the halves tumble away and fall, and seed fluff drifts off; kicking a shell gets a small clack ring too. New small lives, drawn only (the run never sees them): fish that leap from the sea between the dunes and back in with a splash, shadows of fish under the surface, schools leaping far out on the water, butterflies by day in the meadow that lift away as Pip runs by, and bats that flit past his light in the caves. Pip notices them ("A fish!", "Sorry, butterfly!", "Bats! Eek!"). His bubble is smaller, in a regular weight and see-through. It waits until a region's title has faded and keeps clear of the cards (it drops below him, or fades). The ghost's "your best" label fades while the ghost runs alongside Pip, so it never reads as his own. The chapter card fades back while Pip jumps under it, and lines about new things get their article ("Never seen a lift before!").
- **Pip talks (release 28):** now and then Pip says something about what is going on, in a comic bubble over his head that types itself out, with a soft babble when sound is on. He greets each region as he enters it, reacts to the weather rolling in and to a rainbow, calls out the next landmark when it comes into view and cheers when he reaches it, and has something to say about a stomp (more for a chain: "3 in a row!"), a tumble (and a weary one if it keeps happening), growing big or small again, a spring, a splash into the sea, a ferry or lift ride, crumbling boards, smashing a brick, his seed count, nightfall and dawn, the ghost he is racing pulling ahead or falling behind, someone taking over or handing back, a director's trick (and a refusal), and something new to see; in between he chats about the place he is in. Lines are worded from the moment (the landmark's name, the count), the last few of each kind are not said again, small talk waits for a quiet spell and a big moment can cut in, so it comes about every ten to fifteen seconds. It is only shown and heard: nothing in the simulation, the planner or a ghost depends on it. Also: on a phone the row of buttons now shrinks its type to fit (the Journey button ran off the edge).
- **Each place its own scenery (release 27):** the same mountain painting and the same trees had been doing duty everywhere: the snowy range was also the world below the Sky Isles and, darkened and flipped, the caves' walls; pines stood in the meadow as well as on the ridge; the coast borrowed the meadow's rock. Now the Sky Isles look down through the haze on a painted countryside of their own (a patchwork of fields with furrows, woods, a winding river and villages), the caves have walls of their own (layered rock rising into stalagmites and hanging as stalactites, seamed with faintly glowing veins), and the trees belong to their places: oaks and cottages in the meadow, pines on the ridge, pink blossom trees on the sky isles (they shed petals), palms on the coast. Two things had been putting one place's scenery over another's ground: the backdrop began turning into the next region's a whole chunk before the border (so at the end of the coast the frost mountains already filled the sky over the sand), and while a backdrop faded in or out its pines, clouds and islands reset the fade and stood at full strength. The backdrop now blends over the same 16 tiles about the border as the ground, and every alpha inside a backdrop is relative to its fade. `tests/pip-run.test.cjs` checks that each place keeps to its own trees.

- **Smooth on a TV (release 19):** resolution now follows the device. The canvas holds at most 4 million pixels (at twice a 4K TV's density it was asking for about 8 million on every one of its ten or so full-screen passes), and when frames keep coming slower than about 50 a second it renders a step smaller (80%, 64%, then 50% of the pixel density; two steps at once below about 24 fps) and lets the browser scale it up. Each step is judged over 1.5 s of frames after a second for caches to refill; a step that does not make frames faster (a display that only runs at 30 Hz) is taken back and the level settles. The level is remembered on the device for a week. A resolution change goes through the same path as a window resize, so ghosts still replay exactly. In a simulated 4K-density window Chromium went from 29 to a steady 58-60 fps in about 8 s; desktop and phone sizes keep full resolution.

- **Ghost links and the daily world (release 4):** the default world is the day's (UTC), the same for everyone. - **Physics:** fixed 60 Hz with sub-pixel motion; acceleration, skids, variable jump height (hold to jump higher), coyote time and jump buffering.
- **World:** an endless seeded tile world from a chunk grammar (runs, gaps, steps, stump columns, stone pyramids, block tiers). Gap widths come from simulating the jump arc, so every stretch is clearable; difficulty ramps with distance.
- **Creatures:** Mossbud walks and turns at walls; Shellback snail turns at ledges and retreats into a kickable shell that bowls others over; Puddlehop leaps toward Pip on a timer; Puffwing bobs through the air. Stomp them, or bump blocks from below to knock them off.
- **Demo player:** when idle it presses the same buttons a person would. Every few frames it simulates ~40 candidate plans for 1.6 s against the real physics and predicted creature motion (including hopper arcs), then continues each rollout with a default policy (run, and leap where ground ends or a wall/creature is ahead, waiting for headroom under block rows). Average-position scoring and plan commitment stop it from procrastinating. Any key or tap hands control to the player; 9 s idle hands it back.
- **Controls:** ← → / A D to run, Space / ↑ / W / Z to jump. On touch: a pad below the stage.
- **Visible mind:** in demo play, each replan's rollouts are drawn as arcs: white futures, red ones that end in a tumble (with a break), gold for the chosen path.
- **Planner-driven drama:** tension (the share of imagined futures that tumble within 40 frames) eases time down to ~0.55× and pushes the camera in; nothing is scripted.
- **Instant replays:** a 6 s ring of recorded frames; close calls (a lethal option within 12 frames was avoided) and stomp chains replay at half speed with letterbox bars, at most every 30 s.
- **Memory:** each tumble leaves a faint ghost mark. Rollouts near a mark keep a wider berth and avoid retracing it, a "!" shows when Pip approaches one, and a risk level rises after tumbles and relaxes with clean distance (≈2.4 tumbles per 3 min, down from ≈3.6).
- **Phones (portrait):** the stage sits right under the host bar with larger tiles (12–13 across), and a console below it holds a journey strip (chapter, a mini Pip on the track, the ghost, the next landmark and the metres to it), the director tools, Sound/Restart/Share and the hint. Tall phones show the chapter so far (seeds, time, finds, your best today) in the spare space. Where the browser supports it, landings, stomps, springs and tumbles give a light vibration while you play. Landscape keeps the full-height stage.
- **Never stuck:** under a block row the demo leaps only under open sky, running off a lip and jumping in the coyote window when the row reaches the edge. After ~3 s without progress the planner grows impatient and commits to its best attempt instead of waiting forever (a late fall otherwise scores better than an early one). ## Rendering notes

World tiles are 600 world units wide and cached per chunk. Tiles are blitted only up to the next tile's origin so translucent paint (hills, paper grain) never doubles along a seam. Bump the `?v=` query on a script tag when its file changes.

**Pacing.** Small screens draw at 60 fps while a frame costs under 10 ms (30 fps above, returning under 7) instead of always 30. The road's car reflections copy the previous frame before the next one draws; copying the frame still being drawn forced a mid-frame GPU flush (render time 24 → 5 ms). Madhubani's animated peacock feathers clip their hatching by arithmetic (cached per shape) instead of a canvas clip per feather each frame, the same picture at about half the missed frames. Cozy Worlds' steam blurs a small buffer rather than filtering the whole scene each frame (50 → 16.7 ms frames).
