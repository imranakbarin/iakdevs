# Straight traffic fleet

Current production asset: `assets/road-fleet-straight.webp` (1086 x 1448, alpha WebP, 349960 bytes). Generated with built-in ImageGen, then converted to WebP quality 91 with alpha preserved. Original PNG remains in generated_images. Front and rear views cover hatchback, sedan, SUV, pickup, van and box truck.

The prior angled atlas remains in the repository but is no longer used by the traffic renderer. Its fixed three-quarter perspective, combined with lane-based mirroring and shear, made traffic look sideways. The new art is straight front/rear; rendering applies neither mirroring nor shear. Vehicle position and tyre-level anchors follow the shared road projection. Source bounds are measured from alpha components to retain tyres and avoid neighbouring fragments. Lighting/reflections and the bounded 24-entry sprite cache remain in place. Traffic kinematics, lengths and overtaking logic are unchanged.

Native validation: 288 combinations of model, direction, lane, distance and road curve verify no shear or mirroring, exact projected lane centre, tyre-level anchoring and stable paused redraw. Traffic/overtaking regression tests pass. Live browser QA is unavailable.

Row 3 straight REAR views of the same hatchback, sedan, SUV. Row 4 straight REAR views of pickup, van, box truck. Truck cargo box MUST be perfectly rectangular and symmetric viewed front/rear, no diagonal side cargo panel. Rich convincing curved automotive bodywork, crisp lights, mirrored side mirrors, tyres, detailed reflective glass and glossy paint, correct anatomy. No brand logos, no text or markings, blank plates. Each complete vehicle inside the central 80% of its cell horizontally and vertically, with obvious transparent separation from every neighbour. All tyre bottoms at the same baseline per row. Sophisticated automotive illustration rather than toy cubes, match a painted countryside driving scene. True alpha transparency, no background, no ground, no outside shadows. 3:4 aspect ratio, high resolution. Perfectly straight frontal and rearward alignment is more important than any other aesthetic detail.
